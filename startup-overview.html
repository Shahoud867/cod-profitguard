01
Startup idea

A good order should leave
the seller with money left over.

COD means cash on delivery: the buyer pays when the parcel arrives. An order worth PKR 4,500 is not PKR 4,500 in profit. The product, packing, delivery and other costs all take a share.

The problem

Costs and missing payments are easy to miss.

A customer may cancel or give an incomplete address. A parcel may come back. A delivery company (courier) may charge more than expected or send the payment late. Sellers often track all this in separate sheets and delivery websites.

The proposed solution

One place to make and explain each decision.

COD ProfitGuard brings order checks, delivery choices, parcel updates and payment checks together. It shows the cost of each choice and asks a person to approve important actions.

02
The helper team · MAS / game diagram

Small helpers.
One shared picture.

“MAS” means several software helpers working together. Each helper has one job and follows clear rules. They share the same order history, so one helper’s result becomes another helper’s starting point.

03
Target market

Built for the people
running the store.

The first customers are Pakistani online sellers handling roughly 100 to 2,000 cash-on-delivery shipments a month, especially clothing, beauty and small household brands.

01 /

Store owners

They want to know which orders make money, what returns cost and whether couriers pay the right amount.

02 /

Order and support teams

They confirm orders, fix addresses, choose delivery options and help when delivery fails.

03 /

Accounts staff

They compare expected payments with courier statements and follow up on missing or incorrect amounts.

Start locally: approach suitable sellers in Islamabad and Rawalpindi first. Their orders may come from their own website, online shops or social media.

05
Game-theoretic element, in plain words

One delivery choice
changes the next one.

The useful idea here is matching orders and sharing limited delivery slots. Choosing where one parcel goes affects the options and costs left for other parcels.

The seller makes a choice.

The seller decides which courier should carry each order. The aim is to keep delivery costs sensible while meeting the buyer’s needs.

Couriers set the available choices.

Each courier has prices, covered areas and a limit on how many parcels it can take. A cheap courier may not be suitable for every order.

A small, made-up example

Two parcels. One remaining low-cost slot.

Courier ACan take either parcel, but has only one slot left. It costs PKR 200 per parcel.
Courier BCan take only the Lahore parcel. It costs PKR 250. It cannot deliver the other parcel to Peshawar.

Send the Peshawar parcel with A and the Lahore parcel with B. Both parcels get a suitable route, at PKR 450 in total. Giving A’s last slot to Lahore first would leave Peshawar without a suitable courier.

The relevant game idea: matching and sharing limited resources. The software helpers cooperate by sharing information. The route helper weighs the choices together, including delivery charges, possible return costs and available slots. A person makes the final approval.

In the current demo, courier offers stay fixed and delivery outcomes are made up. Price negotiation and couriers changing their offers are possible future additions.

06
Value proposition

Fewer avoidable costs.
Clearer money decisions.

Sellers would use ProfitGuard to understand what is happening, decide what to do next and check whether the results are worth the cost.

✓

Catch problems before sending.

A confirmed cancellation can stop a parcel being sent unnecessarily. An incomplete address can be checked before paying for delivery.

✓

Compare the full delivery cost.

See the delivery fee, possible return costs and other relevant charges together, alongside which couriers can actually take the parcel.

✓

Spot missing or incorrect payments.

Check what a courier should send against what it reports sending. Keep a clear list of differences that need follow-up.

✓

Spend less time piecing things together.

Find order details, delivery updates, reasons for decisions and payment records in one place. Keep people in control of important choices.

Why pay for it? A seller would pay if the time saved, costs avoided and payment errors caught are worth more than the monthly fee. That value must be measured using the seller’s real records. The demo alone cannot prove savings.
